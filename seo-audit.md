Before You Start — How I Use These Prompts
I don’t use Claude to replace my SEO process. I use it to move through each stage faster and think more clearly about what I’m doing.

My workflow goes like this: site audit first, then technical fixes, then keyword research, then competitor analysis, then content, then backlinks. Claude sits alongside every single stage. Here’s exactly how.

1. Site Audit, On-Page SEO, Landing Page and UX
Prompt 1 — Full On-Page and UX Audit

“Act as a sharp, result-focused SEO strategist with expertise in on-page SEO, landing page optimisation, and UX/UI. I’m auditing a [industry] website. Give me a comprehensive checklist covering title tags, meta descriptions, heading structure, internal linking, image optimisation, content quality, keyword placement, page layout, CTA placement, and mobile UX. Format it as an actionable checklist I can work through page by page. Prioritise items by SEO and conversion impact.”

This sets the right tone immediately — you’re not just auditing for rankings, you’re auditing for results. UX and SEO are the same thing in 2026.

Prompt 2 — Technical SEO Triage

“Act as an SEO strategist focused on technical performance. I’m running a technical audit and found these issues: [list issues — 404 errors, 301 redirect chains, slow page speed, missing canonical tags, duplicate content, crawl errors]. Rank these by SEO impact from highest to lowest. Tell me which to fix first, what fixing each one will achieve, and flag anything that could be causing immediate ranking loss.”

Technical audits are overwhelming. You get 20 issues and no idea where to start. This cuts through all of that. Paste the issues in. Get a priority list out. Done.

Prompt 3 — Landing Page SEO and Conversion Audit

“Act as an SEO and conversion rate optimisation expert. Review this landing page brief: [describe your landing page — target keyword, audience, goal, current structure]. Tell me what’s likely missing from an SEO perspective, what UX or layout issues could be hurting conversions, and how to structure the page to rank AND convert. Include recommendations for headline, above-the-fold content, CTA placement, and trust signals.”

Ranking a page that nobody converts on is pointless. This prompt fixes both at once. Wish I had it three years ago honestly.

Prompt 4 — Page Speed and Core Web Vitals Strategy

“Act as a technical SEO specialist. My website scored [X] on mobile and [X] on desktop in Google PageSpeed Insights. Core Web Vitals results: LCP [X], FID [X], CLS [X]. The flagged issues are [list issues]. Tell me which of these directly impact Google rankings versus just user experience. Prioritise fixes by SEO impact and give me a realistic action plan.”

Not every page speed issue is a ranking issue. Developers will tell you to fix everything. Claude tells you what Google actually cares about. Big difference.

Prompt 5 — Internal Link and Site Architecture Review

“Act as an SEO strategist specialising in site architecture. My website covers these main topics and pages: [list your topics and key pages]. Audit my internal linking structure. Tell me which pages should be my pillar pages, which should be cluster pages, how they should link to each other, and what anchor text to use. Identify any orphan pages or pages that are getting diluted link equity.”

Internal linking is the most ignored SEO lever out there. Most sites just link randomly and wonder why certain pages don’t rank. This one builds a proper structure fast.

2. Keyword Research and Search Intent
Prompt 6 — Keyword Suggestion List with Intent

“Act as an SEO keyword research specialist. I’m targeting the [industry] niche for a [type of business]. Give me a keyword suggestion list of 30 keywords covering all stages of the buyer journey — awareness, consideration, and decision. For each keyword include: the keyword, estimated search intent, content format that best suits it, and difficulty level. Group them by intent type.”

30 keywords with intent mapped out. In one go. I still check volumes in Semrush after but the thinking is already done. Saves me at least an hour per project.

Prompt 7 — Topic Cluster Builder

“Act as an SEO content strategist. I want to build topical authority for a [industry] website targeting [main keyword]. Create a topic cluster with 1 pillar page topic and 10 supporting cluster topics. For each topic include: primary keyword, secondary keywords, search intent, suggested content format, and how it connects back to the pillar page. Focus on covering the full buyer journey.”

One conversation. A whole month of content planned. Run this before you even open a keyword tool. Validate volumes after. Not before.

Prompt 8 — Featured Snippet Keyword Finder

“Act as an SEO specialist focused on featured snippet optimisation. I’m creating content for the [industry] niche. Give me 15 keywords or questions that are likely to trigger featured snippets or People Also Ask boxes. Focus on W-questions — who, what, where, when, why, how — and step-by-step queries. For each keyword include the intent, the type of featured snippet it likely triggers, and a 50-word answer optimised for that snippet.”

This is how I’ve ranked in featured snippets consistently. W-questions and step-by-step content. Claude finds the questions and writes the answers at the same time. Genuinely useful.

Prompt 9 — Keyword Segmentation and Intent Mapping

“Act as an SEO strategist. Here is my current keyword list: [paste your keywords]. Segment these keywords into groups by: search intent, topic cluster they belong to, funnel stage (top, middle, bottom), and content format that best matches the intent. Identify any keyword cannibalisation risks where two keywords are competing for the same page.”

Keyword cannibalisation kills rankings silently. Two pages fighting for the same keyword and neither one wins. This catches it before it becomes a problem.

Prompt 10 — Long Tail and Low Competition Keyword Discovery

Act as an SEO keyword specialist. I’m targeting the [industry] niche and want to find quick win opportunities. Give me 20 long-tail keywords with low to medium competition that I can realistically rank for within 3 months on a [new/established] website. Include the keyword, search intent, estimated difficulty, and why it’s a good quick win opportunity. Focus on keywords that could also drive featured snippets or PAA boxes.”

Every project needs quick wins. Clients get impatient. This finds the keywords your competitors have ignored and gives you something to show for early effort.

3. Competitor Analysis
Prompt 11 — Top Ranking Pages and Traffic Analysis

“Act as a competitive SEO analyst. I’m analysing competitors in the [industry] niche. My main competitors are [list competitors]. What types of pages typically drive the most organic traffic for these kinds of websites? What content formats and topics tend to rank best in this niche? What makes the difference between a page that ranks on page 1 versus page 3 in this industry?”

Knowing why a competitor ranks is more useful than knowing that they rank.

Prompt 12 — Content Gap and Opportunity Analysis

“Act as an SEO content strategist. I’m competing in the [industry] niche. What content topics are commonly covered in this industry that I might be missing? What angles or questions do most competitor articles fail to answer well? What content would genuinely serve the reader better than what currently ranks? Give me 10 specific content gap opportunities I can act on immediately.”

The gap between what competitors cover and what they cover well is always where the opportunity is. Every single time.

Prompt 13 — Backlink Profile and Digital PR Competitive Analysis

“Act as a link building and digital PR specialist. I’m analysing the backlink strategy of competitors in the [industry] niche. What types of websites, publications, and platforms typically link to businesses in this space? What digital PR angles work well in this industry — data studies, expert commentary, trend reports, or something else? What types of content earn the most natural backlinks in this niche and why?”

Don’t start building links without knowing what the landscape looks like first. This maps it out before you waste outreach time.

Prompt 14 — Landing Page and Digital Branding Competitive Analysis

“Act as a conversion and SEO specialist. I’m reviewing competitor landing pages in the [industry] niche for the keyword [target keyword]. What elements do high-ranking competitor landing pages typically include — trust signals, social proof, above-the-fold structure, CTA language, brand positioning? What do most competitor pages get wrong from both an SEO and conversion perspective? How can I build a page that outranks and outconverts them?”

Competitors tell you what works and what doesn’t. You just have to look properly.

Prompt 15 — Keyword and Competitive Intent Mapping

“Act as an SEO competitive analyst. I’m targeting these keywords in the [industry] niche: [paste keyword list]. For each keyword tell me: who is likely ranking (big brands, small sites, or a mix), what type of content is ranking, how competitive this keyword is, and what angle I should take to differentiate my content from what already ranks.”

Spend months targeting a keyword dominated by huge brands and you’ll get nowhere. Know the landscape before you commit.

4. Content Creation
Prompt 16 — SEO Blog Post and Article Outline

“Act as an SEO content strategist and copywriter. Create a detailed outline for a blog post targeting the keyword [your keyword] with [informational/commercial] intent. The audience is [describe audience]. Include: a click-worthy headline, an opening hook, H2 and H3 subheadings, key points to cover under each section, internal linking suggestions, a featured snippet target section, and a recommended word count. The tone should be [casual/professional/authoritative].”

If writing speed is the problem, not the SEO side, I have 11 prompts specifically for that. Faster drafts, better hooks, cleaner edits.

Every article I write starts here. Two minutes for the outline. Then I write the actual thing. Structure first. Always.

Prompt 17 — Product and Service Page Copy

“Act as an SEO copywriter specialising in product and service pages. I’m writing a page for [product/service name] targeting the keyword [target keyword]. The audience is [describe audience] and the goal is [rank and convert/primarily rank/primarily convert]. Write the full page copy including: SEO headline, subheadings, key benefit sections, trust signals, FAQ section optimised for featured snippets, and a closing CTA.”

Product pages that only rank but don’t convert are a waste. This prompt keeps both goals in mind from the start.

Prompt 18 — Press Release and Digital PR Content

“Act as a digital PR specialist and SEO copywriter. I want to create a press release for [topic/announcement/data/study] for a [industry] brand. The goal is to earn coverage and backlinks from [type of publications]. Write a full press release with: a newsworthy headline, a strong opening paragraph, key facts and data points, a quote from a spokesperson, boilerplate company description, and contact details. Make it genuinely newsworthy — not promotional.”

Most press releases are just ads. Nobody covers ads. Newsworthy first. Company stuff second. This prompt gets that right.

Prompt 19 — Guest Post Pitch and Article Outline

“Act as a content strategist and outreach specialist. I want to write a guest post for [type of publication] in the [industry] space. Step 1: Write a guest post pitch email under 150 words that focuses on value for their audience not what I want. Step 2: Write a full guest post outline for the topic [your topic] targeting [keyword] with the following angle: [your angle].”

Pitch and outline in one prompt. The pitch part especially — most guest post emails get ignored because they lead with what the writer wants not what the editor needs.

Prompt 20 — Meta Titles, Descriptions and Schema Markup

“Act as a technical SEO and copywriting specialist. I have a [type of page] targeting the keyword [your keyword]. Write: 5 meta title variations under 60 characters each with different angles (curiosity, benefit, question, urgency, direct), 5 meta description variations under 155 characters each, and the recommended schema markup type for this page with a brief explanation of why it helps SEO.”

Used to take me 20 minutes to write meta tags I was actually happy with. Now it takes 2. Five options. Pick the best one. Move on.

5. Backlink Strategy and Digital PR
Prompt 21 — Backlink Opportunity Mapping

“Act as a link building strategist. I’m building backlinks for a [industry] website that publishes [type of content]. Give me 15 specific types of websites, publications, directories, communities, and platforms that would be most likely to link to this type of content. For each source include: why they would link, how to approach them, what type of content to pitch, and the estimated link quality.”

Knowing where to build links before you start is the difference between productive outreach and wasted weeks.

Prompt 22 — Digital PR Campaign Ideation

“Act as a digital PR strategist with SEO expertise. I want to create a digital PR campaign for a [industry] brand that earns natural backlinks from authoritative publications. Give me 5 campaign ideas that are genuinely newsworthy — data studies, industry surveys, trend reports, expert roundups, or creative campaigns. For each idea include: the angle, target publications, why it would earn coverage, and what type of content to produce.”

Digital PR is the best link building strategy going. High quality links. Real coverage. But only if the idea is actually newsworthy. This prompt generates ideas that editors want to cover.

Prompt 23 — Guest Post Outreach Strategy

“Act as a link building and outreach specialist. I want to build backlinks through guest posting for a [industry] website. Give me: a list of 10 types of publications that accept guest posts in this niche, a personalised outreach email template under 150 words that doesn’t sound like a template, 5 guest post topic ideas that would appeal to each publication type, and tips for following up without being annoying.”

Guest posting works. Just not how most people do it. This covers the whole thing — who to target, what to pitch, how to follow up.

Prompt 24 — Broken Link Building Campaign

“Act as a link building specialist. I want to run a broken link building campaign in the [industry] niche. Explain the exact process step by step — how to find broken links on relevant sites, how to identify what content to replace them with, and how to write an outreach email that gets a response. Give me an outreach email template and explain what makes broken link outreach work when most people’s emails get ignored.”

Broken link building has one of the highest response rates in outreach. Most people still overcomplicate it. This simplifies the whole process.

Prompt 25 — Personalised Link Building Outreach Emails

“Act as an outreach and digital PR specialist. I want to send personalised link building outreach emails to [type of site] in the [industry] niche. The content I want them to link to is [describe your content]. Write 3 different outreach email variations — one for cold outreach, one for warm outreach where I’ve engaged with them before, and one for resource page link requests. Each email should be under 150 words, genuinely personalised in tone, and focused on why linking helps their audience.”

Cold. Warm. Resource page. Three situations. Three different tones. Personalisation is what separates a reply from being ignored. This gets that right.


One Last Thing
These prompts only work if you know what you’re doing.

Claude doesn’t replace years of SEO experience. It just makes that experience move faster. If you paste Prompt 6 into Claude without knowing what keyword intent means — you’ll get a list you don’t know how to use.
